Inventory Is Growing While Transportation Capacity Contracts

According to FreightWaves, the October Logistics Managers' Index recorded inventory levels of 66.9, up 8 points from September. That represented the fastest inventory growth in four years. Growth also accelerated during the second half of the month, suggesting that the inventory buildup became more pronounced as October progressed.

Transportation capacity moved in the opposite direction. FreightWaves reported a capacity reading of 38, marking an 11th consecutive month of contraction. Transportation prices registered 88. The article explains that the index measures expansion or contraction: readings above 50 indicate expansion, while readings below 50 indicate contraction. These figures should therefore be read as index readings, rather than percentage changes in freight bills.

For an inventory owner, the practical planning issue is the combination of those trends. Consider whether a proposed purchase has a receiving plan, an identified storage requirement and an outbound destination. If the purchase depends on freight arrangements that remain unresolved, flag that dependency before committing to an arrival schedule. The survey gives businesses a reason to examine those assumptions; it does not establish the availability or cost of an individual shipment.

Carrier Rate Targets Call for a Budget Review

FreightWaves reported that truckload carriers expect to pursue double-digit rate increases during the upcoming bid season. Knight-Swift Transportation was among the carriers describing that objective in annual contract negotiations. Surveyed logistics managers also expected transportation conditions to remain tight, with future readings indicating continued capacity contraction and stronger pricing growth.

Keep the distinction between a carrier's negotiating target and a shipper's actual agreement clear. The article describes carrier expectations and survey responses. It does not establish a universal increase for every route, shipment or transportation arrangement. A business should avoid applying a reported rate target automatically to its entire shipping budget.

Instead, review the assumptions attached to upcoming freight movements. Identify which shipments have agreed transportation terms, which need updated quotes and which depend on dates or quantities that may change. Give particular attention to inventory purchases whose economics rely on an older freight estimate.

For businesses evaluating 3PL services in Miami, bring those assumptions into the planning discussion. Describe the intended arrival schedule and outbound shipment requirements alongside the storage request. Ask what information is needed to assess the work, and keep any unconfirmed transportation costs visible in the purchasing decision.

More Inventory Also Requires a Warehouse Plan

The pressure described by FreightWaves extended beyond transportation. Warehouse capacity registered 43.5, remaining in contraction even though the pace of decline slowed. Warehouse prices reached 80.7 after increasing 7.2 points. Inventory costs also rose to their highest index reading since March 2022.

The article reported different conditions across the supply chain. Upstream companies, including manufacturers and wholesalers, reported severe warehouse capacity contraction, while downstream firms reported no change in space availability. That distinction matters when interpreting the headline: the survey did not describe an identical storage situation for every business.

Use the report as a prompt to build a specific inventory request. List the products expected to arrive, their quantities, their storage requirements and their intended release schedule. Separate inventory allocated to existing orders from inventory purchased in anticipation of holiday demand. Where the sales timing remains uncertain, make that uncertainty explicit when discussing storage needs.

Also review the work required between arrival and shipment. Identify whether goods need unloading, inspection, labeling, bundling or order fulfillment. A pallet count alone does not describe those requested tasks. Putting them into the receiving brief gives the warehouse discussion a clearer scope and helps the business assess its own proposed inventory flow.

Connect Receiving Decisions to Outbound Requirements

FreightWaves described firms accelerating inventory purchases in hopes of strong holiday sales, despite transportation and tariff costs. Those hopes are part of the reported explanation for the buildup. They should not be treated as confirmation that a particular seller's additional stock will sell on its intended schedule.

Before increasing inbound quantities, map the next steps for each product group. Specify whether the inventory is intended for individual ecommerce orders, wholesale shipments or distribution to Latin America. Record any preparation requested before release, and distinguish confirmed customer commitments from tentative sales plans.

Turn the Market Report Into a Specific Service Request

The broad lesson from the FreightWaves report is a planning concern: inventory growth is occurring alongside limited capacity and rising logistics cost readings. A useful response is to review the details a business can specify before requesting warehouse and fulfillment services.

  • Inbound inventory: Describe expected products, quantities, arrival timing and unloading needs.
  • Storage: State product requirements and the anticipated storage period, identifying any timing that remains uncertain.
  • Preparation: List requested inspection, labeling, kitting or bundling work.
  • Outbound orders: Separate ecommerce fulfillment, wholesale distribution and export-related distribution needs.
  • Open assumptions: Identify freight quotes, clearance timing or customer release instructions that still need confirmation.

Use that brief to compare the proposed purchase with the work needed to receive and distribute it. If an arrival date changes, revisit the related release plan rather than leaving the original assumptions in place.
