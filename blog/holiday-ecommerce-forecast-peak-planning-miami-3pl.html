2026 Holiday Ecommerce Forecasts: Planning Notes for Miami 3PL Users

Forecasts point to steady online growth, deep Cyber 5 discounts and late shoppers, which puts pressure on inventory timing and fulfillment cutoffs.

By Miami Alliance 3PL Team | November 20, 2026 | 9 min read

Digital Commerce 360 reported that forecasts for the 2026 holiday shopping season call for ecommerce sales to grow at a rate similar to 2025. The Cyber 5 runs from Thanksgiving Day on Nov. 26 through Cyber Monday on Nov. 30, and the report draws on projections and surveys from Adobe, Deloitte, ICSC, the National Retail Federation and Salesforce covering sales, discounts and shopper behavior. For online sellers that store and ship goods through a Miami warehouse, the numbers point to a steady but compressed peak where inventory timing and fulfillment cutoffs carry real weight.

The Cyber 5 Calendar Puts Demand Into a Short Window

Digital Commerce 360 reported that the Cyber 5, the period that includes Thanksgiving Day, Black Friday and the three days that follow, begins on Nov. 26 in 2026 and concludes on Cyber Monday, Nov. 30. Shopper surveys cited in the report suggest that window will be busy.

According to results published by ICSC (formerly the International Council of Shopping Centers) on Nov. 18, 88% of 1,007 U.S. adults surveyed planned to shop at some point during the Cyber 5, and 77% expected to spend the same or more than they did in 2025. The National Retail Federation (NRF) released its own survey results on October 28 showing that 64% of shoppers expected to wait until Thanksgiving to do most of their holiday shopping in 2026, up from 51% in 2020.

For a fulfillment operation, that combination matters more than any single sales figure. When a larger share of buyers waits for one long weekend, orders tend to bunch up instead of spreading evenly across November and December. Inventory that is still in transit, sitting at a port or waiting to be labeled when the Cyber 5 starts may miss the days when shoppers are most active.

What Adobe and Deloitte Expect From Holiday Sales

Adobe's forecast for the final two months of 2026 calls for online sales to grow 6.7% year over year to reach $275.1 billion, Digital Commerce 360 reported. That pace would be comparable to, but slightly below, the 6.8% growth Adobe measured in 2025.

Deloitte's wider forecast, published in September, covers total holiday retail sales, including ecommerce and physical stores, from November 2026 through January 2027. It set expectations at $1.70 trillion to $1.71 trillion, which would mean growth of 4.0 to 4.8% from a year earlier, after the prior period saw 4.1% growth.

The signal for sellers is steadiness rather than a surge. The report noted that consumers continue to face pressure from inflation, tariff-related costs and other macroeconomic factors. In a season expected to look much like last year, execution is one of the levers a seller controls directly: having stock in place, keeping counts accurate and shipping promptly once a shopper commits.

Discounts Started in October and Peak During the Cyber 5

Deep discounts began early this year. Online shoppers spent $9.86 billion on Oct. 6 and 7 during Amazon's Prime Big Deal Days, according to Adobe. Looking ahead, Adobe's forecast says discounts during the Cyber 5, which Adobe refers to as Cyber Week, would reach up to 30% off listed prices.

Adobe anticipates discounts of 21% in the days immediately preceding Thanksgiving, then 10% to 15% during December, roughly in line with the 2025 holiday season. Price remains a deciding factor. "Sales and discounts (56%) are the most cited factors in shoppers' decision-making around which retailers to shop throughout the holiday season," wrote Lea Grgich, a senior research analyst at the NRF.

"Shoppers are increasingly willing to switch and/or try new brands in order to find the best value - making Thanksgiving Weekend a prime opportunity for retailers to earn their business," said Tom McGee, president and CEO of ICSC.

Discount calendars drive warehouse calendars. A promotion that attracts new customers only pays off if the promoted items are on hand and the orders leave on time. Sellers planning bundles, gift sets or multi-pack offers for the deepest discount days need those kits assembled before the Cyber 5, and should keep stock available for December promotions, when Adobe expects discounts to settle into the 10% to 15% range.

Shopping Assistants and Agents Are Changing How Orders Arrive

The report also highlights a shift in how shoppers find products. Online retailers are serving a rising share of traffic referred by automated shopping assistants, along with software agents that browse ecommerce sites on behalf of human users. In an October survey by Digital Commerce 360 and Bizrate Insights, 26% of online shoppers said that comparing prices and finding deals was their primary reason for using these tools when buying products.

Adobe projects that this referral traffic to online retailers' websites will be up 159% on Thanksgiving, 95% on Black Friday and 88% on Cyber Monday from 2025 levels. Adobe analysts also reported that these referred shoppers are converting better: in August, Adobe Analytics said such referrals generated 53% more revenue per visit than other shoppers, and in September it observed the gap at 83% per visit, the largest it had seen in 2026. Separately, Salesforce predicted that agents would be responsible for 20% of all 2026 holiday ecommerce traffic, and that 1 in 3 ecommerce sites would use personal, site-specific agents to serve shoppers during Cyber Week, which Salesforce defines as the Tuesday before Thanksgiving and the six days that follow.

For fulfillment, the practical point is that shoppers arriving through these tools increasingly come ready to buy at the best price they can find, which can sharpen order spikes around the moment a deal goes live. The report notes that content and catalog data play a role in retailers' pursuit of traffic and conversion. On the warehouse side, that data is only as useful as the inventory counts behind it.

A Peak-Season Checklist for Online Sellers

Taken together, the forecasts describe a season with steady growth, deep but familiar discounts, and demand concentrated around a single long weekend. Several planning steps follow from that picture:

  • Position stock before the Cyber 5. Inventory should be received, counted and available to sell before Nov. 26, with extra buffer on the items planned for the deepest discounts.
  • Build kits and bundles early. Gift sets and multi-packs take labor to assemble. Doing that work ahead of the peak frees capacity for picking and packing when orders arrive.
  • Know the daily cutoff. Holiday shoppers judge sellers on ship dates. Confirm the order cutoff time and the warehouse schedule for the holiday week, and set customer-facing delivery promises to match.
  • Prepare each sales channel. Marketplace orders, such as Amazon, TikTok Shop or Mercado Libre, often carry their own labeling and prep requirements. Sort those out before promotions begin.
  • Plan for returns. Promotional and gift orders can bring returns after the holidays. Decide in advance how returned items will be inspected and put back into sellable stock.
  • Keep listings and counts aligned. With shoppers comparing prices and options quickly, an oversold item or an inaccurate listing can cost both the sale and the customer.
