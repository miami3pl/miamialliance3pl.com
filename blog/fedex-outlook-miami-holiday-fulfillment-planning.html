What FedEx Changed in Its Forecast

According to Transport Topics, FedEx raised its adjusted earnings forecast for 2026 to $18 to $18.60 a share. Its previous forecast had topped out at $18.10. The company also projected revenue growth of about 12%, while quarterly adjusted earnings reached $3.74 a share as revenue and operating margin improved.

Transport Topics described the stronger outlook as an encouraging sign for parcel carriers heading into the holiday shipping season. It also reported that UPS had raised its sales and profit outlook earlier in the week. Together, those announcements form the industry context for the story; they should not become a substitute for a merchant's own order forecast.

For a business owner, the useful distinction is between reported carrier performance and the work required to prepare goods for shipment. Treat the earnings announcement as a reason to revisit your plan. Compare expected orders with the inventory you intend to make available, and identify which products still need receiving, inspection, labeling or bundling before they can enter your fulfillment workflow.

Build the Inventory Plan Around Your Own Orders

Transport Topics said the improved FedEx outlook suggested shipping demand was holding strong ahead of the busy year-end months. For your business, use that assessment as context rather than a purchasing instruction. Base any inventory decision on your own sales expectations, available goods and commitments to customers.

Start with a product-level review. Separate inventory already available for fulfillment from goods awaiting arrival or preparation. For each incoming shipment, ask your warehouse contact to confirm the information needed to receive it and the work expected before orders can be released. For imported goods, include the customs-clearance status in that discussion rather than assuming arrival and availability mean the same thing.

  • Identify products intended for individual ecommerce orders.
  • List goods reserved for wholesale orders or marketplace preparation.
  • Flag items that need inspection, labeling, kitting or bundling.
  • Assign responsibility for communicating changes to arrival plans.

Use the resulting list to discuss priorities with your fulfillment provider. Keep decisions tied to specific products and orders, and revisit them when your own information changes. A broad carrier forecast should remain separate from a decision to buy or release additional stock.

Specify the Work Between Receiving and Shipping

A useful response to the FedEx news is to review the instructions that accompany your inventory. Before releasing a holiday promotion or a new batch of orders, document what should happen between receiving the goods and handing completed packages to the carrier. Ask your provider to review those instructions against the services you need.

For individual orders, specify the products, quantities and packing instructions. For bundles, define the contents and how substitutions should be handled. For wholesale shipments, provide the applicable retailer requirements. If goods are intended for marketplace preparation, separate that work from direct customer fulfillment in your instructions and inventory plan.

Keep Network News Separate From Delivery Commitments

Transport Topics reported that FedEx was continuing a broad overhaul to merge its historically separate air and ground delivery networks. The publisher also said FedEx had spun off its freight division and sold its supply chain unit as part of a focus on its core parcel business.

For a merchant, these developments provide background for conversations about shipping arrangements. Avoid turning the reported network changes into an assumption about the timing of your own packages. Ask for the information relevant to your account, shipment requirements and planned order releases before publishing customer-facing delivery language.

Review the handoff between fulfillment and transportation as a distinct part of your plan. Ask who confirms that an order is ready, how carrier collection is coordinated and how an exception should be communicated. Keep warehouse preparation expectations separate from the delivery estimates you present to customers.

For a promotion, write down the shipping assumptions behind the offer and identify who will review them before launch. If an assumption remains unresolved, settle it with the responsible contact. The practical use of the news is to prompt specific operational conversations, with decisions grounded in the arrangements that apply to your business.

Give Each Sales Channel a Clear Inventory Plan

If your business serves multiple sales channels or destinations, review those commitments together before assigning holiday inventory. Identify goods intended for U.S. customer orders, wholesale distribution, marketplace preparation or Latin America distribution. Decide which inventory can be reassigned and who has authority to make that decision.

Keep the discussion specific to the work involved. A product list alone may leave instructions unresolved, so include the intended destination, preparation requirements and release decision for each group of goods. Ask your provider to confirm the scope before treating the plan as ready for execution.
