Higher container volumes and freight pricing pressure put receiving schedules, inventory priorities and distribution plans in focus for Miami businesses.

Container Growth Comes With Higher Pricing Pressure

FreightWaves reported that global container volumes reached 17.35 million TEUs in September 2026, up 8% from a year earlier. North American imports increased 9%, with Asia and Europe supplying the main sources of growth. The report described that regional increase as an acceleration from the annual growth recorded in the second quarter.

Pricing moved upward as well. According to FreightWaves, the Global Price Index reached 123 points in September, rising 5.1% from August and standing 62% above its September 2025 level. The article linked pricing pressure to shipping route disruption, constrained capacity and higher operating costs.

For an importer, the useful distinction is between the market indicator and the shipment being purchased. The reported index offers context for a freight discussion; it does not establish the price of a particular movement into Miami. Review current shipment quotes alongside the receiving and distribution work planned for the goods, and keep the assumptions behind each decision explicit.

Keep Regional Data Separate From Local Receiving Decisions

The geographic scope of the report matters. FreightWaves described North American import growth and global shipping conditions. It did not provide a Miami-specific container volume figure or establish a receiving delay at PortMiami or Port Everglades. Businesses should avoid treating the regional increase as evidence that their own cargo will arrive late.

FreightWaves also reported that port congestion and high terminal utilization threatened the industry's ability to clear backlogs, citing Container Trade Statistics. That is a reason to examine shipment information carefully, rather than assign an unsupported delay to every inbound container.

For businesses buying 3PL services in Miami, start with the details attached to each shipment: its latest expected arrival, customs clearance status and intended warehouse handling. Ask which details remain provisional and which have been confirmed. If the arrival expectation changes, review the receiving plan and any outbound commitments tied to that inventory. Use the news as a prompt for that review, while basing operational decisions on the cargo's actual status.

Prepare Receiving Instructions Before Cargo Arrives

Translate the market discussion into a practical receiving brief. Specify what is arriving, how the shipment is packed and what should happen after unloading. If some goods are intended for storage and others for immediate distribution, make that distinction clear in the instructions shared with the warehouse.

Consider reviewing these items with the receiving team:

  • The expected product and carton details, including any differences from the original shipment plan.
  • The goods that need inspection, labeling, bundling or other preparation before fulfillment.
  • The inventory intended for ecommerce orders, wholesale distribution or onward shipment.
  • The contact responsible for communicating changes to arrival expectations and customs clearance status.

Connect Inbound Inventory With Its Intended Sales Channel

The report's import figures describe cargo movement, not demand for a particular seller's products. A business should therefore review its own orders and inventory priorities before changing purchasing plans in response to the headline. Higher regional imports alone do not establish that a specific product needs a larger replenishment order.

For each inbound shipment, identify the intended destination of the stock. Separate goods allocated to customer orders from goods intended for future inventory, and specify any preparation required before release. Where the same product serves several sales channels, establish allocation priorities before asking the warehouse to fulfill against it.

The planning question is which handling steps each inventory allocation needs, rather than whether the broader import increase justifies changing every part of the fulfillment operation.

Review Import and Export Plans Separately

FreightWaves reported that every region recorded higher imports in the third quarter than in the same period of 2025. Export performance was less uniform: North American export volumes fell 1% year over year during the quarter. These different results make it important to distinguish inbound and outbound trade when interpreting the story.

For a business importing goods for U.S. sales while also distributing into Latin America, review each shipment's purpose separately. An inbound inventory plan should identify where the goods will be held and prepared. An outbound distribution plan should identify the destination, allocated products and handling instructions. Neither plan should assume that a regional trade percentage describes the business's own order flow.
