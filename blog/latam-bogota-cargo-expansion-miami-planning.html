Latam's Bogotá Cargo Expansion Puts Miami Planning in Focus

A planned cargo terminal in Colombia gives Miami importers and distributors a reason to review shipment handoffs, receiving requirements and inventory plans.

By Miami Alliance 3PL Team | November 12, 2026 | 7 min read

Latam Airlines Group plans to build and finance a new cargo terminal at El Dorado International Airport in Bogotá, Colombia, FreightWaves reported. The project would expand handling space and support the airline's flower export business. For businesses using Miami as a distribution point, the announcement is a reason to review how cargo plans connect with warehouse receiving and customer orders.

What Latam plans to change in Bogotá

According to FreightWaves, Latam Airlines Group will build and finance a new cargo terminal at El Dorado International Airport. The planned 46,400-square foot facility would triple the airline's existing work space and enable faster processing of import and export shipments. FreightWaves described the project as part of a broader plan to upgrade cargo handling infrastructure at Latam's primary operating hubs.

The flower business is a central part of the announcement. FreightWaves reported that the facility would expand Latam's cold storage capacity by about 20%. The report also cited Latam's statement that it holds a 35% share of the flower export market. Those figures describe Latam's operation and the planned expansion.

Latam also plans to expand its domestic cargo facilities in Bogotá and establish a dedicated space for incoming imported cargo, according to FreightWaves. A business following the project should therefore look beyond the flower headline and ask which announced changes relate to its own freight. Treat the announcement as a planning signal, then seek shipment-specific confirmation before adjusting purchase commitments or customer delivery expectations.

Connect the expansion to your own shipment plan

For an importer, exporter or distributor, the practical question is how the planned terminal relates to goods already ordered or being considered for purchase. Start by identifying the shipments that would actually use the relevant cargo operation. Avoid applying the announcement across an entire inventory plan simply because a supplier is based in Colombia.

Ask the airline or freight contact to clarify what the project means for the proposed shipment, including cargo acceptance arrangements and the information needed before arrival. Keep those answers separate from assumptions about warehouse receiving in Miami. The airline's planned processing improvements should be evaluated alongside the arrangements for each subsequent handoff.

  • Identify the goods, quantities and shipment references covered by the booking.
  • Request confirmation of the expected arrival and release arrangements.
  • Give the receiving warehouse the packing information and handling instructions relevant to those goods.
  • Define which inventory should be stored and which inventory should move toward an outbound order.

For businesses evaluating 3PL services in Miami, this approach turns a regional infrastructure announcement into a concrete receiving discussion. It also gives the business a clearer basis for deciding whether any purchasing or distribution changes are appropriate.

Use the flower headline to review handling requirements

FreightWaves connected Latam's planned cold storage expansion directly to growth in the flower export market. That detail matters when interpreting the announcement: the reported investment addresses a particular cargo need. A seller of electronics, cosmetics or other goods should evaluate its own handling requirements rather than use the flower operation as a model for every product.

Prepare a written inventory profile before discussing storage. Include the product description, packaging, handling instructions and any documented environmental requirements. Ask prospective service providers to confirm how those requirements fit the proposed service. Keep the discussion specific enough to distinguish the conditions needed during transportation from those requested for storage and order preparation.

For mixed inventory, consider preparing separate instructions for products with different needs. Identify which items require special review before receipt and which can follow the agreed general receiving process. Avoid using a broad label such as specialty inventory as a substitute for product-level information.

The useful takeaway is to connect each handling request to the actual goods. The terminal announcement can prompt that review, but the service decision should rest on the inventory profile and confirmed arrangements.

Prepare shipment information before warehouse receiving

FreightWaves reported that Latam is pursuing more digital data exchange with industry participants and customs authorities. The report also described RFID-based tracking tools for flower shipments and the CargoLink platform for advance cargo acceptance in Colombia. These initiatives sit alongside the airline's physical expansion.

For a business owner, the corresponding planning task is to make shipment information usable by the people receiving and distributing the goods. Review the product identifiers on the packing list against the identifiers used for inventory and customer orders. Where they differ, provide a clear mapping before receipt rather than leave the receiving team to interpret unfamiliar labels.

Build the receiving instructions around decisions the warehouse will need to make. Specify whether goods are intended for storage, order fulfillment, wholesale distribution or another agreed service. Include instructions for reporting a mismatch between the expected shipment and the goods presented for receipt.

For goods requiring customs clearance, establish who will communicate release status and authorize the receiving handoff. Keep that communication connected to the shipment reference. The practical aim is a shared set of instructions that the business, its freight contacts and its warehouse can review before the cargo reaches the next stage.

The Miami training update puts handoffs in focus

The FreightWaves story also covered Latam Cargo's deployment of virtual reality training at its Miami hub. According to the report, the initial phase covers about 550 employees, including 250 forklift operators. The training scenarios address cargo handling, pedestrian traffic, machinery separation and dangerous goods segregation.

That is a separate operational development from the planned Bogotá terminal. For a warehouse buyer, it provides a useful prompt to discuss the procedures surrounding a shipment, alongside the space available to hold it. Ask how receiving instructions reach the people handling the goods and how an exception should be escalated to the business.

Make those discussions concrete. Describe what should happen if a product label does not match the paperwork, if packaging needs review or if the shipment includes inventory requiring handling approval. Assign a business contact who can answer questions about the goods and document any decisions that change the original instructions.

For Latin America distribution from Miami, apply the same discipline to outbound preparation. Confirm the destination, order contents and required preparation before asking the warehouse to move inventory into the next stage.

Before changing inventory commitments, confirm which shipments are affected, what handling they require and what the receiving warehouse needs to know. Then match the warehouse work to the intended sales channel.
