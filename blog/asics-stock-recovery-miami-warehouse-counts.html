Asics Inventory Recovery Puts Miami 3PL Stock Checks in Focus

More frequent inventory counts at Asics offer a practical starting point for reviewing stock availability with a Miami warehouse provider.

For businesses choosing a Miami warehouse provider, the story raises a practical issue: how stock discrepancies are found, reviewed and corrected before they affect an order.

The Business Issue Behind Faster Inventory Recovery

Digital Commerce 360 reported that Asics uses Corvus Robotics drones to find merchandise that has become misplaced within its primary North American distribution center. Before the deployment, misplaced items could go undetected for up to six months, according to an Asics distribution executive cited in the report. That created the possibility of accepting an order for merchandise employees could not locate.

The reported improvement concerns recovery speed: Asics estimated it was finding misplaced inventory roughly 60% faster. That figure should not be read as an order-accuracy rate or a result another warehouse can expect. It describes the company's experience with this deployment.

For a business reviewing outsourced fulfillment, use the example to define the problem you want addressed. Ask how the warehouse distinguishes stock recorded in its system from stock employees can locate and pick. Request a walkthrough of what happens when an item appears available but cannot be found, including who investigates and how the business receives an update.

Look at the Review Process Alongside the Count Schedule

According to Digital Commerce 360, the drones count inventory monthly in selected Asics storage areas, compared with twice annually before the deployment. They scan product codes and count boxes at storage locations. Corvus said the system compares that information to identify discrepancies, which an employee reviews the following morning using captured images and video.

The report describes a process that includes both detection and human review. Asics employees can examine the footage before deciding whether someone needs to visit a location. Some corrections can be made from a desk, according to an inventory control manager cited by the publication.

When discussing a warehouse agreement, ask for the same level of clarity about responsibility. Establish which inventory gets counted, what prompts an additional check and who may approve a correction. Ask how unresolved differences are documented. A count schedule is useful information, but your review should also cover the steps between finding a discrepancy and updating the inventory record.

Include Returns and Shared Stock in the Discussion

Digital Commerce 360 reported that Asics uses the drones in a singles area holding returned merchandise and individual pairs of shoes, and in a reserve area holding full cartons. The distribution center serves ecommerce, company stores and wholesale customers from a shared inventory pool. It also processes customer returns.

During one three-month period, the drones identified 26 misplaced cases containing more than 200 pairs of shoes, according to the report. Asics said those goods could otherwise have remained unavailable for sale until the next scheduled inventory review. The example connects location accuracy with the decision to make merchandise available.

If your business uses a warehouse for both consumer orders and wholesale distribution, ask how each channel draws from available stock. Include returned items in that conversation: establish what review you require before a return becomes available again and how its location should be recorded. For a proposed wholesale 3PL arrangement, also explain your carton and individual-unit handling needs so the inventory discussion reflects the way you sell.

Treat Marketplace Availability as a Separate Decision

According to Digital Commerce 360, Asics previously withheld between 12 and 15 units out of every 100 from the inventory feeds it supplied to marketplace partners. Corvus said the company used that approach because merchandise listed as available might be missing or difficult to locate when an order arrived.

With monthly counts providing greater visibility, Asics said it now publishes its full inventory availability feed. Corvus linked the change to the ability to offer more merchandise at full price. However, the companies did not disclose how much additional revenue resulted, the publication reported.

For your own business, review the evidence behind any stock withheld from sales channels. Ask whether the decision reflects an unresolved location problem, a pending review or a deliberate allocation choice. Agree on who can change availability after a discrepancy is resolved. The Asics example supports examining that decision; it does not establish that every seller should publish every unit or remove its existing inventory allowances.

Turn the Story Into a Practical Warehouse Review

Digital Commerce 360 reported that the deployment also changed how Asics employees use their time. According to the companies, employees could spend more effort on reconciliation, discrepancy investigations and wholesale-support projects instead of routine counts. For a prospective customer, that makes the follow-through worth discussing alongside the counting method.

Use a service review to request a clear explanation of the process your inventory would follow. Bring examples from your own orders and returns, and work through these points:

  • Counting scope: Identify which storage areas and inventory categories would be included in scheduled checks.
  • Exception handling: Clarify who investigates a missing item and how unresolved cases reach your team.
  • Record corrections: Establish what evidence and approval you expect before inventory quantities change.
  • Sales availability: Define who decides when recovered or returned goods can be offered again.

Use the answers to compare the proposed service with your operating needs. Keep the review focused on responsibilities, records and communication rather than assuming a particular piece of equipment will produce the same outcome reported by Asics.
