A Shared Portal for Airport Cargo Communication

Miami International Airport reported that its new Airport Cargo Community System, or ACCS, was developed by Kalé. The airport described a shared portal for freight forwarders, cargo handlers, truckers, carriers and ground handlers. Its stated purpose is to reduce reliance on fragmented, manual information exchange across that network.

According to the airport, cargo trucks currently arrive with limited information about arrival times and cargo types. MIA identified that information gap as a contributor to congestion, emissions and inefficiency. The platform is intended to improve information sharing, advance planning and truck scheduling.

For an importer or distributor, the useful starting point is to ask the parties handling airport cargo how the new system will affect their communication. Establish who will provide pickup updates, what information those updates will contain and who should receive them. Treat the launch as a reason to review those arrangements before building a warehouse receiving plan around expected improvements.

Keep Projected Benefits Separate From Shipment Plans

MIA reported handling 3.4 million U.S. tons of freight in 2025. According to the airport, its cargo volume requires a daily average of 190 cargo-only flights and more than 1,600 truck trips. Those figures explain the scale of the operation the shared platform is intended to support.

The airport also described potential environmental and operating savings if ACCS were adopted across its entire cargo operation. Its estimates use assumptions about reduced paper handling and truck turnaround time. These are conditional projections in the announcement, rather than measured results from the newly launched system.

Apply that distinction to purchasing and inventory decisions. Ask for shipment-specific pickup information before adjusting a receiving appointment or a distribution plan. When reviewing the platform with a freight provider, distinguish between capabilities that are available for your shipment and benefits expected from broader adoption.

For warehouse planning, use confirmed updates as the basis for decisions. Keep any expected improvement in airport waiting time separate from the delivery timing communicated for a particular load.

Review the Handoff From Airport Pickup to Warehouse Receipt

The operational opportunity described by MIA centers on communication and scheduling. Businesses can use those same subjects to structure a review with their freight and warehouse contacts. Start with a specific inbound shipment and map the updates needed between the airport pickup plan and the warehouse receiving plan.

  • Identify the update owner. Name the contact responsible for communicating the expected pickup and any subsequent change.
  • Agree on shipment details. Ask what cargo information the warehouse needs to prepare for receipt, and decide who will supply it.
  • Confirm the receiving arrangement. Match the expected delivery with the receiving hours and arrangements agreed with the warehouse.
  • Set a change process. Decide who should be contacted if the pickup or delivery plan changes.

Keep customs coordination explicit in that discussion. Miami Alliance 3PL coordinates receiving of goods after customs clearance with the client's licensed customs broker. For a shipment headed to the company, include that receiving coordination in the plan alongside airport pickup updates.

The practical aim is a clear set of responsibilities. Ask each participant to explain which updates it supplies and which decisions remain with the business owner.

Connect Receiving Decisions With the Next Use of Inventory

MIA said ACCS will also serve as foundational digital infrastructure for connections with its international partner airports, supporting the movement of goods along specific international trade lanes. That is part of the airport's stated direction for the platform. Businesses should ask their freight contacts what information is available for the particular route they use.

For a business selling in the United States or Latin America, use the receiving discussion to specify what should happen after goods reach the warehouse. Identify inventory intended for ecommerce orders, wholesale distribution or another outbound movement. Give the warehouse instructions tied to that intended use rather than leaving the next step undecided.

Where airport pickup timing remains uncertain, ask the warehouse how changes should be communicated before revising outbound instructions. Keep the inbound status and the requested distribution work clear in the same planning conversation.

What to Review With Your Logistics Contacts

The airport's announcement gives business owners a concrete topic for their next logistics review: how shared cargo information will reach the people making receiving and inventory decisions. Use a current or planned shipment to make the discussion specific.

  • Ask whether the parties handling that shipment are using ACCS and which updates they can share.
  • Request a clear distinction between an expected pickup time and a confirmed pickup update.
  • Agree on the contact responsible for passing delivery changes to the warehouse.
  • Review the instructions for storage, preparation and distribution after receipt.
  • Ask how the parties will assess any change in waiting time or coordination for your shipments.

For businesses evaluating Miami 3PL services, bring those questions into the service discussion. Describe the goods, the expected inbound movement and the work required after arrival. Ask for a receiving arrangement that reflects those needs and the information your freight contacts can actually provide.

Use the airport's stated goals as discussion points, while keeping individual shipment decisions tied to the updates supplied by the parties handling the goods.
