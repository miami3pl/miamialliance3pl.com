Colombia Ecommerce Air Cargo Squeeze Shapes Miami 3PL Planning

The Loadstar reports rising ecommerce demand into Colombia, giving businesses a reason to review inventory releases and cargo handoffs from Miami.

Colombia's ecommerce imports are putting pressure on inbound air cargo space, according to The Loadstar. The publication reported growing shipment demand alongside capacity figures that point to a tighter market. For businesses distributing goods from Miami, the practical response is to review how warehouse preparation, inventory releases and confirmed air transport fit together before committing goods to a departure.

The demand and capacity figures tell different stories

The Loadstar reported that an airfreight wholesaler estimated Colombia's weekly ecommerce imports at 3,000 to 4,000 tonnes, with support for that estimate from LATAM Cargo's market intelligence. Separately, LATAM said its own imported ecommerce volumes on the US-Colombia corridor increased 53% in January-August compared with the same period last year. That airline-specific result should be read separately from the broader market comparisons.

According to figures from Rotate supplied to The Loadstar, ecommerce demand into Colombia increased 16.8% in the latest three-month comparison with the preceding period. Overall cargo demand rose 1.1%, while capacity declined 6.7%. Against the equivalent period last year, ecommerce demand grew 11.9%, overall demand increased 5.7% and capacity expanded 4.7%.

The distinction matters for planning: the report describes both growing ecommerce demand and capacity that has not kept pace with that demand. Treat those figures as a reason to verify space for a proposed shipment. They do not establish the availability of a particular flight or the departure date for an individual order.

Review the Colombian gateway before releasing inventory

According to LATAM estimates reported by The Loadstar, approximately 99% of Colombia's ecommerce import tonnage enters through Bogotá, Medellín, Cali and Barranquilla. LATAM also said it was developing direct import services into regional gateways to shorten delivery times and improve network efficiency. The report does not establish that every destination or proposed shipment has access to those services.

For a business planning shipments from Miami, use the intended Colombian destination as a starting point for discussions with the transport provider. Request confirmation of the available gateway, the proposed routing and the next handoff after arrival. Avoid assuming that a network development described in the news applies to an existing booking.

Then connect that routing discussion to the warehouse release instructions. Specify which goods belong to the confirmed movement and which should remain in storage pending further direction. Businesses reviewing their broader operating plan can also explore Latin America distribution from Miami alongside their transport arrangements.

Separate warehouse readiness from transport availability

The Loadstar's report concerns pressure on air cargo space. For businesses using 3PL services in Miami, translate that news into a review of release decisions: which inventory should be prepared, which shipment has confirmed transport and who can authorize a change. Do not treat completion of warehouse preparation as evidence that air capacity has been secured.

Before sending release instructions, review the proposed shipment against current customer commitments. Identify goods that require immediate attention and goods that could remain in storage if the transport plan changes. If a partial shipment is under consideration, decide which orders or products it would cover before authorizing warehouse work.

  • Identify the inventory assigned to each proposed departure.
  • Confirm the transport arrangement before authorizing the corresponding release.
  • Give the warehouse clear instructions for goods awaiting a revised departure.
  • Assign responsibility for communicating changes to warehouse and transport contacts.

These are planning steps, not a forecast of disruption. The article's market figures support closer attention to availability; they do not establish that every shipment to Colombia will face a delay.

Make the handoff instructions specific

For a proposed Colombia shipment, review the work requested from the warehouse separately from the movement requested from the transport provider. State whether the goods need storage, picking and packing, kitting or another preparation step. Then identify the instruction that authorizes their release. This gives the business a concrete decision to revisit if the proposed departure changes.

Ask the warehouse and transport contacts to confirm the details relevant to their part of the shipment. For example, request confirmation of the goods to be prepared, the intended collection arrangement and the contact responsible for revised instructions. If the business changes the order allocation, communicate that change before authorizing the next warehouse step.

Keep unconfirmed explanations out of the operating plan

The Loadstar also reported suggestions that some Colombian imports might be moving onward to neighboring markets. Those suggestions remained unconfirmed. The wholesaler raised Venezuela and Brazil as possible onward destinations, while LATAM said the imports it processed into Bogotá were intended for Colombian consumers.

That distinction should remain intact when interpreting the story. The report supports a discussion of strong ecommerce demand into Colombia. It does not establish that redirected shipments explain the increase. Likewise, LATAM's market intelligence estimate of approximately 5% month-on-month cross-border ecommerce demand growth this year is a reported trend, not a forecast for an individual seller.

Base inventory commitments on the business's own orders, intended destinations and confirmed transport arrangements. Use the news to identify what needs checking, rather than to assume a new market opportunity or a particular disruption. If a distribution proposal depends on onward movement beyond Colombia, verify that proposal independently before making it part of warehouse release instructions.
