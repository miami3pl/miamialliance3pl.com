The planned purchase puts cross-border parcel routing and returns on the review list for businesses managing fulfillment from Miami.

A Florida acquisition aimed at cross-border parcels

According to FreightWaves, Evri has agreed to acquire Cross Border Connect, based in Boca Raton. The report describes the Florida company as a specialist in ecommerce delivery and returns between the United Kingdom and the United States. FreightWaves presents the transaction as another step in Evri's international expansion and its entry into the U.S. delivery market.

The distinction between an agreement and a completed acquisition matters when reviewing the news. Treat this announcement as a development to monitor. Before assigning orders to a proposed service, request confirmation of its availability, the applicable routes and the party responsible for each part of the shipment.

FreightWaves reported that financial terms were not disclosed and that Cross Border Connect co-founder Chris Lentjes would remain as CEO, according to Evri. For a business owner, the more useful follow-up is operational: ask what, if anything, would change in booking, pickup, tracking and returns for the shipments the business actually sends.

Look closely at the handoff into domestic delivery

FreightWaves describes Cross Border Connect as operating an asset-light network supported by a proprietary technology platform. According to the report, that platform manages pickup and customs clearance at six ports of entry, followed by direct injection of packages at the carrier's closest hub. The report says this approach allows retailers and marketplaces to optimize routing by cost, speed and service.

Use that description to build a specific service review. Ask the prospective provider to map the proposed journey from pickup through customs clearance and the domestic carrier handoff. Request an explanation of where shipment status becomes visible and who handles a package when its movement does not match the plan.

  • Confirm which origins and destinations the proposed service covers.
  • Identify who books pickup and supplies shipment information.
  • Ask who manages customs clearance and communicates clearance issues.
  • Establish who investigates a delivery exception after the carrier handoff.

Keep this review tied to actual products and destinations. Do not assume the announcement establishes a suitable route for every order in a Miami fulfillment operation.

Evaluate the proposal against your own shipping needs

FreightWaves quoted Evri's chief commercial officer describing expected improvements in cross-border delivery speed, costs and shipping flexibility. Those statements describe Evri's expectations for the acquisition. They do not establish a delivery outcome or a shipping price for an individual seller.

For a useful comparison, prepare a representative shipment profile and request a proposal based on it. Include product descriptions, package dimensions, shipment weight, origins, destinations and the intended returns arrangement. Ask the provider to identify the assumptions behind its proposal and explain which responsibilities belong to the seller.

Review the proposal alongside the warehouse work each order would require. Would goods arrive as inventory for later fulfillment, or would packages already be prepared for onward delivery? Would the warehouse need different labels or packing instructions? Resolve those points before revising operating procedures. If a limited trial is appropriate, define what to review afterward, including tracking visibility, handoff records, delivery exceptions and the handling of returned goods.

Give returns the same attention as outbound parcels

Returns are part of Cross Border Connect's specialization, according to FreightWaves. Businesses assessing the planned acquisition should therefore review the return journey alongside outbound delivery. Ask whether the proposed arrangement covers only the movement of a returned parcel or also any handling after it reaches its destination.

Prepare written instructions for the warehouse and delivery provider. Specify the return destination, the information needed to match an item to an order and the person authorized to decide its next step. Ask each provider to confirm which instructions it can carry out and how it will report exceptions.

  • Define how the returned item should be identified.
  • Specify any inspection requested before an inventory decision.
  • Assign authority for restocking or further review.
  • Agree on the information the seller needs to resolve the customer's return.

Miami Alliance 3PL offers returns processing. When discussing that service, bring the proposed carrier return flow and the seller's handling instructions so the requested warehouse work can be clearly defined.

Keep Latin America plans separate from route assumptions

The FreightWaves report centers on Cross Border Connect's U.K.-U.S. specialization and Evri's international expansion. It does not establish a new Latin America delivery service. A business selling into both the United States and Latin America should verify each intended route rather than extend the announcement's scope.

For a Miami inventory plan, list the destinations and sales channels the warehouse would support. Then identify the requested work for each flow: ecommerce pick, pack and ship, wholesale order preparation, or distribution to another market. Request confirmation of the carrier arrangements for each destination before changing inventory allocations.

Miami Alliance 3PL offers U.S. and Latin America distribution from Miami, ecommerce fulfillment, and wholesale and B2B distribution with retailer compliance. Businesses considering these services should describe their own order flows and ask how the requested warehouse tasks would be organized. Keep the warehouse discussion specific to those needs, while evaluating the parcel acquisition on the routes and capabilities reported by FreightWaves.
