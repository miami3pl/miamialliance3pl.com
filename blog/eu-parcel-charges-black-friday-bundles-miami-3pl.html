EU Parcel Charges Test Black Friday Bundles: Lessons for Miami 3PL Users

The Loadstar reports that EU customs charges counted per declaration line are changing the math on bundles, with lessons for how Miami sellers build kits, keep product data clean and place inventory.

What Changed in the EU and Why the Timing Matters

The Loadstar reported that EU imports of low-value ecommerce goods fell 63% year on year in the quarter immediately after the bloc introduced its €3 customs duty in July. A €2 handling fee has been in force since 1 November, on top of that duty.

According to The Loadstar, the EC's preliminary assessment counted 555 million low-value ecommerce items entering the EU in Q3, down from 1.51 billion a year earlier. The same assessment said some goods appeared to be coming in as bulk shipments for storage and fulfilment within the bloc. Those figures cover the period before the 1 November fee, so they do not yet show the effect of the second charge.

The timing puts the change in the middle of peak season. This year's Black Friday, on 27 November, will be the first since the EU removed its duty exemption for low-value parcels. That means the biggest promotional window of the year is also the first real test of how the new charges land on discounted orders.

Why Black Friday Bundles Feel the Charges First

The charges are counted per line, not per box. The Loadstar reported that where both charges apply, each distinct line on the customs declaration adds €5. Its example: a jumper, a candle and a mug declared on three lines would incur €9 in duty and €6 in handling fees, before VAT is added. Seko Logistics warned UK retailers that some Black Friday bundles could incur €15 in customs charges, also before VAT.

"The retailers that will feel it most are the ones whose best-selling deals are bundles," said Steve Aylott, Seko's UK and Ireland sales director. "Five euros per product type sounds small, until it is sitting on a £20 Black Friday deal," he added.

Aylott laid out three paths for retailers: absorb the cost, build it into European prices, or rethink the bundle. He also pointed to the risk of a surprise at delivery: "What they can't afford is a customer finding out after they've paid."

The lesson reaches beyond Europe. A bundle that looks like one product to a shopper can be several products on paper. When charges are tied to declaration lines, every added product type can carry its own cost, which makes bundle design a pricing decision as much as a merchandising one.

Origin and Product Data Decide the Bill

The Loadstar reported that the origin of the goods can also change what is owed. Goods that qualify for UK "preferential treatment" can avoid the €3 duty if they are entered on a full customs declaration and VAT was collected outside the EU's Import One-Stop Shop. The €2 handling fee still applies in that case, and it is non-refundable if the goods are returned.

"Where a parcel ships from and where the goods come from are two different questions," said Tim Harding, Seko's UK and Ireland customs manager. "Customs looks at origin and classification." He warned: "If the product data behind a Black Friday order is wrong, the duty will be wrong too, and peak is the worst possible time to find out."

For any business that sells across borders, that comment points straight at the records behind each SKU. Classification and origin questions are matters for the seller and its licensed customs broker. The warehouse side of the job is making sure the physical inventory matches the records those answers rely on. A kit assembled differently from its listing, or a unit mislabeled on the shelf, can turn a correct customs file into a wrong one.

The non-refundable handling fee on returns is worth noting too. When a returned item leaves behind a cost that does not come back, the return rate on a bundle becomes part of its real margin.

Air Cargo Capacity Is Moving

The change is already visible at air cargo gateways. The Loadstar reported that Liège Airport said small-parcel volumes fell 42% after the July duty took effect, reducing the number of dedicated ecommerce flights. Total Q3 cargo tonnage there was virtually flat year on year, at 334,467 tonnes.

WorldACD said Hong Kong-Europe air cargo chargeable weight, across all commodities, was still down 32% year on year in the week of 2-8 November, according to the same report.

The topic also came up in Miami. At TIACA's Air Cargo Forum in Miami in October, Rotate's Ryan Keyrouse identified Liège and Budapest as European ecommerce gateways hit by falling demand. He said that across the affected Europe-bound traffic, other commodities had not replaced the lost ecommerce volumes, as they had after changes to US de minimis rules. "You see that no other commodity picked up to backfill that," he said. According to The Loadstar, airlines instead redirected capacity from Europe towards North America and intra-Asia markets.

For shippers in the Americas, the report shows how a customs change in one region can shift air capacity toward others, including North America.

Stock Placement and the Next Rule Changes

Seko said that for retailers with steady EU demand, holding stock inside the bloc could make sense, allowing them to import in bulk and fulfil orders locally. The Loadstar noted that Shein, which opened its Wrocław logistics hub in December 2025, said this month it was investing €500m this year to expand the operation. That matches the EC's observation that some goods appeared to be entering as bulk shipments for storage and fulfilment.

More changes are coming. The UK government plans to scrap customs duty relief on imports worth £135 or less by October 2028, six months earlier than originally planned. Goods will become subject to standard UK tariff rates, and the government plans a separate fee to cover the cost of processing low-value imports. The Loadstar reported that ministers have yet to set the fee or decide whether to charge it per parcel or per item.

Parcel demand itself has not disappeared. J&T Express reported a record 136m parcels across its global network on 12 November, the day after China's Double 11 shopping event, and averaged 119m parcels a day from 1 to 12 November, up 26% year on year. The orders are still there. The open question for sellers is where the stock sits when an order is placed and how each shipment is declared. For companies that rely on 3PL services in Miami to serve U.S. and Latin American buyers, that same question applies closer to home.
